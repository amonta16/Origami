## The story in one line

A real owner can't show clients what a treatment will do → the tool that can costs thousands → we put it on every
client's phone and made it bring them back → we took it to that owner, she ran it on her own phone, and we asked
if she'd pay.

The structure: **her problem → our fix → her verdict.** Marison opens, carries the middle and closes. The 3D
explains what the camera can't show.

## How this hits the Startup Launch rubric

- **Problem and who has it:** a named owner says it in her own words (M1), then VO2 adds the cost.
- **Who would pay:** med spa owners pay, clients use it free (VO8).
- **How we tested the market:** an in-person visit to a real spa, where she ran the live product on her own phone (block 6).
- **What we learned:** she needs to try it in her workflow and see the cost against the return (M5, VO8).
- **Proof:** her yes on camera (M5), plus unprompted reactions to the scan and the scratch reward (M2, M3, M4).

## Smoothness rules for the whole cut

- **No straight cuts anywhere.** Every join is a dissolve of 6–24 frames, a match (light to light, phone to phone,
  bars to bars, warm card to warm room) or a J/L-cut, where sound leads or trails the picture.
- **Frame rate:** master at 30p. INT is 24p: conform the drone with optical-flow retiming so it doesn't judder.
  Talking-head shots can use plain frame blending.
- **Color bridge:** cool her shadows slightly and add the same soft vignette the 3D world has, so warm amber and
  cool navy feel like one film. Two-up and end-card backgrounds use the 3D navy.
- **Easing:** every scale or position move uses ease-in-out, with no linear moves.
